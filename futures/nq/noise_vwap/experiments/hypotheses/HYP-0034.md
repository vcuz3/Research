## Proposed mechanism

The paper's thesis: a *fast-decaying alpha* (a short-horizon predictor whose net
edge is destroyed by its own turnover) is not worthless — it can be *informational
alpha* that improves the execution of a slower *monetizable alpha*. Their fast
signal is a 5-minute mean-reversion cue (fade the last bar's sign). Their
monetizable base is an intraday open-anchored ATR-band breakout with a stop back
at the session open — i.e. essentially THIS project's Noise-Area/VWAP breakout
archetype. They report the overlay lifts the base Sharpe 0.87 -> 0.99 (+~200 bps
CAGR) net of costs by:

- **Entry**: after a breakout is confirmed, postpone entry until a fast-alpha
  *pullback* prints (a 5-min bar opposite the breakout direction), aiming for a
  better entry price.
- **Exit**: after the stop condition triggers, postpone the liquidation until a
  fast-alpha *bounce* prints (a 5-min counter-move in the position's favour),
  aiming for a better exit price. The structural stop is unchanged; only its
  timing is refined.

Mechanism claim: waiting for a micro-pullback/bounce converts a
liquidity-consuming breakout entry/stop into a mildly liquidity-providing one,
improving average fill prices net of cost.

## Exact change

Single default-off overlay on the frozen continuous-stop baseline
(`core.engine2`, bands lb90, RTH, 30-min Concretum decision clock, VWAP entry
gate, every-bar band/VWAP stop, next-open fills, explicit costs). New args:
`fast_overlay`, `fast_release`, `fast_horizon`, `fast_entry`, `fast_exit`,
`fast_fixed_delay`, `fast_hazard`, `fast_seed`. When `fast_overlay=False` the
engine is **bit-exact** the baseline (asserted).

Overlay skeleton (identical machinery across the real arm and every control — only
the *release trigger* differs, mirroring the EXP-0043 discipline):

- Fast alpha at bar `i` = sign of the trailing `fast_horizon`-minute return
  `close[i] - close[i-h]`, known at the close of bar `i`. All fills stay
  **next-open** (rule 1/2), so we never credit the pullback bar's own close.
- **Entry**: when the clock produces a breakout `want` while flat (or on the
  new-side leg of a flip), arm a pending entry of side `want` instead of entering.
  On each later bar, release (enter next-open) when the trigger fires. A pending
  entry that never releases before the daily flat is dropped (no trade).
- **Exit**: when the band/VWAP stop first triggers (non-flip), arm a pending exit
  instead of closing. On each later bar, release (close next-open) when the
  trigger fires. The EOD flat always closes regardless. Flips and the mandatory
  daily flat are never delayed.

`fast_release` policies (the release trigger, everything else held fixed):

- `opposite` — **the real overlay (the paper)**: entry releases on an opposite
  fast bar (pullback); exit releases on a favourable fast bar (bounce).
- `same` — **inverted control**: entry releases on a same-direction fast bar; exit
  on an adverse fast bar. If the "improvement" is symmetric in the fast sign, the
  overlay is not using the claimed mean-reversion information.
- `fixed` — **fixed-delay control**: release exactly `fast_fixed_delay` bars after
  arming, ignoring the fast alpha. Calibrated to the real arm's mean achieved
  delay. If a blind fixed wait reproduces the gain, the fast alpha carries no
  timing information.
- `random` — **DECISIVE control (random-release null)**: release on a coin flip at
  per-bar hazard `fast_hazard`, calibrated (bisection) to reproduce the real arm's
  trade count. Keeps the wait, its persistence and exposure; destroys only the
  information. This is the EXP-0043 control-1b design.

## Primary metric

Zero-trade-day daily net-ATR-R Sharpe uplift `dSharpe` of the overlay over the
baseline on the common post-lookback sample (`score_candidate`), reported for NQ
and ES, alongside net R, gross pt/trade, hit rate, maxDD, and recent (>=2023)

## Kill test (preregistered)

The overlay is **REJECTED** unless ALL hold on the PRIMARY market (NQ):

1. `dSharpe >= +0.10` over baseline **AND** net R does not fall (project
   `UPLIFT_GATE`; a wash/negative real pass is already a REJECT).
2. It **beats the random-release null**: `frac(random dSharpe >= real dSharpe)
   < 0.05` at matched trade count. (Decisive — this is where the project has
   repeatedly found "waiting/less exposure" machinery, e.g. the random-exit
   control in `fx-mandatory-stop-and-random-exit-control` and EXP-0043 control 1b.)
3. It **beats the fixed-delay control** by a clear margin (real dSharpe above the
   fixed arm), i.e. the fast alpha adds over a blind matched wait.
4. The **inverted (`same`) arm does not do as well or better** — a symmetric
   result falsifies the mean-reversion mechanism.

Only if 1-4 pass on NQ do we spend the drift-preserving **Null C** (gate-nullc
rule) and require ES transfer with the same sign.

## Rationale and evidence

Directly relevant priors in this workspace, all of which the paper omits:

- **Random-exit equivalence** (`fx-mandatory-stop-and-random-exit-control`): an
  "exit only after it reverts" rule was statistically indistinguishable from a
  random exit at the same rate. The random-release null here is the same test.
- **EXP-0043 control 1b**: for a persistent/stateful timing rule, randomise the
  release TRIGGER at matched exposure — a one-shot trade drop cannot express it.
- **Shared-close / touch-vs-fill** (LEARNINGS §6; project note in EXP-0043): a
  large fraction of minutes open at the prior close, so "enter at the pullback"
  can be the same number that ended the displacement — an artifact, not a fill.
- **Quality-not-alpha turnover levers** (`nq-es-crossmarket-confirm`, Hurst/VEI
  series): better per-trade selection with less exposure repeatedly nets to no
  risk-adjusted gain here. A +0.12 Sharpe overlay is exactly this magnitude.

Prior probability of a genuine, null-surviving edge is low; the value of the run
is a clean, claim-matched test of a published result on our own audited engine.

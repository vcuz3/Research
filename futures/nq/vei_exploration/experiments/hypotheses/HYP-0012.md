# HYP-0012 — The forward-vol core is a TIMING instrument: it correctly calls changes away from trailing 30-minute volatility

- Status: confirmed, with EXP-0012 qualified (EXP-0015, 2026-07-31)
- Created: 2026-07-31
- Origin idea: `experiments/IDEA_BACKLOG.md` item 16 (raised by EXP-0013's closing note)
- Builder: Claude
- Proposed mechanism: EXP-0012 showed the two-input core beats the causal same-slot
  median by a large within-slot margin, i.e. it beats the SEASONAL benchmark. It has
  never been tested against the other trivial benchmark a practitioner would actually
  use: "the next thirty minutes will look like the last thirty minutes." Only where the
  forecast DISAGREES with recent volatility can it change a decision, so all of its
  usable content lives in the disagreement set. If the model's predicted change from
  trailing volatility correlates with the realised change, it is a timing instrument; if
  not, it is a well-calibrated restatement of persistence and seasonality.
- Exact change: no strategy change and no model change. Define the naive persistence
  benchmark `P = past_rv30_bp`, the SAME estimator as the target applied to the window
  ending at the decision bar's own open (a strict backward twin, so `log(fwd/P)` is
  unbiased by construction). With `F` the frozen walk-forward core forecast and `A` the
  realised forward 30-minute RV, measure whether the predicted log-change
  `pc = log(F/P)` predicts the realised log-change `rc = log(A/P)`.
- Frozen baseline: `P` itself — the zero-skill "volatility persists" forecast. The causal
  same-slot median `M` is carried as a third leg so the three-way comparison
  (persistence / seasonality / model) is visible in one table.
- Primary metric: WITHIN-SLOT Spearman IC between `pc` and `rc`, count-weighted across
  slots, with a 90% session-block-bootstrap CI, on both markets. Co-diagnostics: log-MSE
  and MAE skill of `F` against `P` overall and by disagreement decile; the expansion and
  contraction sides reported separately.
- Kill test: treat the core as a CALIBRATION instrument only — accurate, but adding
  nothing a persistence-plus-seasonality rule does not already give — unless, on BOTH NQ
  and ES: (i) the within-slot IC between `pc` and `rc` is positive with its 90% CI
  excluding zero, AND (ii) the EXPANSION side alone (`pc > 0`) is individually positive
  with its CI excluding zero. Condition (ii) is the discriminating one and is stated in
  advance: volatility mean-reverts, so calling a contraction after a spike is nearly free,
  and a pass driven entirely by contraction calls is not evidence of timing skill.
- Required controls:
  - Rule-23 reproduction of the EXP-0011 notebook pooled ICs before use, at 5e-4.
  - Executable proof that `P` is causal (never reaches past the decision bar's open) and
    that it is the same estimator as the target, so the comparison is not a scale artefact
    — pinned in `tests/test_forward_vol.py`.
  - Within-slot throughout, so a per-slot scale difference between `P` and the forward
    window cannot manufacture or destroy the result.
  - Skill by |disagreement| decile: if skill FALLS as the model disagrees more, the model
    is overconfident exactly where it would be used, which is a distinct and reportable
    failure from having no skill at all.
  - A degenerate control: the same statistic for the same-slot median `M` in place of `F`.
    If seasonality alone calls changes from persistence just as well, the model adds
    nothing beyond the free benchmark on this axis either.
  - Per-year stability, NQ and ES replication, rule-9a coverage accounting for every row
    the benchmark drops.
- Data scope: NQ and ES clean Databento RTH 1-minute. Annual expanding-window
  walk-forward with first tested year 2016 through 2026-07-14. `P` is undefined where the
  trailing window is broken by a missing minute, a roll or a symbol change, and the
  forward target is undefined at the 15:29 and 15:59 slots. Consumed history.
- Multiple-testing note: one primary statistic per market plus one preregistered
  conditional (the expansion side). The decile table, the era split and the `M` control
  are declared diagnostics of the same claim, not separate discoveries.

## Rationale and evidence

This is the question EXP-0013 left standing. That run closed the application of the
forecast to an existing directional book; it did not ask what the forecast knows that a
trivial rule does not. EXP-0012 answered half of it — the model beats the seasonal
benchmark decisively — but the seasonal benchmark is not what a practitioner would use as
a default. Persistence is.

The disagreement framing matters because a forecast that agrees with persistence changes
no decision no matter how accurate it is. Splitting by the SIGN of the disagreement is
what separates a genuine timing instrument from mean-reversion arithmetic, and the
expansion side is both the hard case and the tradable one: an anticipated volatility
expansion is the input to widening a stop, standing aside, or buying optionality, whereas
an anticipated contraction mostly recovers what the market has already told you.

## Outcome

**CONFIRMED on both markets, including the discriminating expansion-only gate**, by
`EXP-0015`. Within-slot IC(predicted log-change, realised log-change) = +0.3766
[+0.3690,+0.3841] NQ and +0.3901 [+0.3819,+0.3984] ES; expansion side alone +0.2201
[+0.2050,+0.2351] and +0.2380 [+0.2240,+0.2520]; marginal value over the same-slot
median's own call +0.1363 / +0.1383. Skill rises monotonically with disagreement size.

The run's larger result is a qualification of EXP-0012: **persistence is a far harder
benchmark than seasonality.** Within-slot IC vs realised RV is model +0.8808/+0.8740,
persistence +0.8674/+0.8610, seasonality +0.4186/+0.3830, so EXP-0012's ~+0.47 delta over
the same-slot median is +0.013 over trailing 30-minute RV. The levels are nearly all
persistence; the deltas are where the model lives. Honest caveat: on expansion calls the
ranking works but log-MSE skill over persistence is only +0.008 / +0.061. See
`artifacts/runs/EXP-0015/review.md` and FINDINGS section O.

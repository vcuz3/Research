# HYP-0002 — Study D's momentum switch is a volatility regime, not a time-of-day clock

- Status: **H1 CONFIRMED — Study D survives the audit (outcome 3, full survival)**
  (EXP-0005). Declared pre-run; kill test written before execution (rule 24).
- Created: 2026-07-26
- Origin: audit of Study D (EXP-0003) / Study E (EXP-0004), prompted by a planning
  diagnostic showing Wilder VEI has a strong deterministic time-of-day profile.
- Builder: Claude (Opus 5)

## Proposed mechanism (the claim under test)

Study D reports corr(past 30m ret, next 30m ret) ≈ 0 in low/calm VEI and **+0.104 NQ /
+0.096 ES when VEI > 1.10**, and EXP-0004 confirmed the sign at the strategy level. The
claim on the record in `reports/FINDINGS.md` and `MEMORY.md` is that **VEI selects when
momentum is present** — a *volatility regime* statement.

There is an untested alternative explanation. VEI here is an intraday, session-reset
ATR(10)/ATR(50); the long leg is anchored to the volatile open, so VEI drifts upward
monotonically through the session (NQ mean 0.745 at 10:30 → 1.165 at 15:59). The fixed
cut `VEI > 1.10` is therefore a ~1% event in the morning and a 19–64% event late, and
roughly two thirds of Study D's high-VEI sample sits in the 14:29/14:59/15:29 slots. No
script in this project has ever grouped by time of day. If intraday momentum is simply
stronger late in the session, `VEI > 1.10` would reproduce Study D's result while
carrying no volatility information at all.

**H1 (the project's current claim):** the high-vs-rest momentum contrast survives when
time of day is held fixed.
**H0 (the alternative):** the contrast is a clock effect and vanishes within slot.

## Exact measurement

`scripts/s4_term_structure.py` (descriptive; no trading, no fills, no costs, so no Null
C — rule 17 does not apply to a measurement with no P&L claim). Canonical VEI
Wilder(10/50), 30-min decision clock, NQ and ES, same sample as EXP-0003.

The decisive statistic (F3c) is the **count-weighted within-slot correlation contrast**:
compute corr(past 30m, fwd 30m) separately for high-VEI and rest *inside each of the 13
decision slots*, then average across slots weighted by count. Averaging only after
conditioning on the slot removes slot composition entirely. Session-block bootstrap
(resample whole sessions) for the 90% CI on the difference.

Supporting measurements: F1 term structure (H = 5…120 and to-close), F2 Lo-MacKinlay
VR(q) of the forward path by regime (an independent estimator), F3a/b the per-slot VEI
profile and *unconditional* per-slot correlation, F3d raw level vs causal same-slot
percentile rank **at matched selection count**, F4 robustness over past_win ∈ {10,30,60}
and (short,long) ∈ {(5,20),(10,50),(10,100)} at matched selectivity.

## Primary metric

Within-slot high-minus-rest correlation difference at H=30, with its session-block
bootstrap 90% CI, on **both** NQ and ES.

## Kill test (preregistered)

1. **The audit.** If the within-slot contrast collapses to ≈0 (90% CI includes 0) on
   **either** market, H0 wins: Study D is substantially a time-of-day effect. Then
   `reports/FINDINGS.md` and `MEMORY.md` must be rewritten to mark Study D qualified or
   superseded, and Study E's interpretation revised with it (rule 25 — a NO-GO is a
   successful outcome; the dead claim must not be left looking current).
2. **Partial survival.** If the contrast keeps its sign and its CI excludes 0 on both
   markets but is materially smaller than the pooled +0.104/+0.096, Study D stands as
   *qualified*: real, but with its effect size overstated by slot composition. The
   honest number replaces the headline.
3. **Full survival.** Contrast retains sign, significance, and roughly its size on both
   markets → Study D stands as written.

The unconditional per-slot correlation (F3b) is reported alongside, because a strong
slot profile with a surviving within-slot contrast is the case where BOTH effects are
real and additive — that is a distinct, and more interesting, outcome than either alone.

## Required controls

Cross-market: ES must reproduce the sign (the sibling is the mechanism test). Matched
selection count in F3d (comparing normalisations at equal threshold reads only a
selectivity dial — LEARNINGS 2026-07-20, quantile companion). Matched selectivity in F4
so cells differ only in the feature. Rule 9a coverage table by slot.

## Data scope

NQ + ES clean Databento RTH 1-min, 2011–2026, ~3710 sessions — the same consumed
research history as EXP-0001…0004. Not a sealed holdout.

## Multiple-testing note

The primary is the single within-slot contrast at H=30, the horizon Study D itself used.
F1/F2/F4 are labelled supporting/robustness and are not eligible to become the headline.
F3d compares exactly two normalisation schemes at one matched count.

## Note on F3c's estimator

Within-slot conditioning uses full-sample slot membership, which is a two-sided
statistic. That is legitimate here because F3c is an **attribution/measurement** control,
not a signal construction — it never selects a trade. The causally-constructible version
of the same idea is F3d's trailing same-slot percentile, which is strictly past-only and
is the one that could ever be traded.

## Verdict (EXP-0005)

**H1 confirmed, outcome 3 (full survival) on both markets.** The time-of-day
concentration is real and large — 88.9% (NQ) / 87.4% (ES) of high-VEI decisions sit in
the 14:00-and-later slots, which are 31% of the clock — but it is not the cause. The
count-weighted within-slot contrast is **+0.1098 [+0.0251, +0.1715] (NQ)** and
**+0.1033 [+0.0208, +0.1681] (ES)**, retaining 106% / 105% of the pooled Study D
difference, with CIs excluding 0 on both. Stable across the `MIN_CELL` threshold, and
still +0.073 / +0.068 when only the four largest late slots are used. Study D stands as
written in `reports/FINDINGS.md`.

Three further results came out of the same run:

- **De-seasonalising VEI destroys information.** At matched selection count a causal
  same-slot percentile rank is weaker than the raw level on both markets and its CI
  includes 0 (NQ +0.069 vs +0.100; ES +0.068 vs +0.086). VEI's information is in its
  ABSOLUTE level; time of day is neither the cause nor a useful normalisation.
- **The momentum information decays with horizon** (peaks at 5–10 min, gone by 90–120),
  which *corrects* EXP-0004's "the effect strengthens with hold" reading — the H=60
  net-P&L gain was cost amortisation, not signal.
- **VR(q) shows no regime difference** once time of day is matched, so "momentum regime"
  means a drift tilt, not a smoother forward path.

Separately, the H=60 estimand erratum (rule 13) is recorded in
`artifacts/runs/F_term_structure/review.md` and in HYP-0001; it does not change the
EXP-0004 verdict.

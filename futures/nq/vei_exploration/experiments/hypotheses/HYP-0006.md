# HYP-0006 — Normalising VEI by its trailing same-slot mean gives a threshold that means what it says, at no information cost

## Proposed mechanism

A-corrected (b) established that this VEI is not centred on 1 and drifts monotonically
upward all session (mean by slot 0.745→1.165 NQ / 0.788→1.216 ES), because the
session-reset long ATR stays anchored on the volatile open while the short one walks off
it. Consequently **`VEI > 1.10` is a late-session selector wearing a volatility label**:
it fires on ~1% of morning decisions and 19–64% of late ones, and 88.9% NQ / 87.4% ES of
high-VEI decisions sit after 14:00. That is a calibration defect, not a subtlety — the
label's meaning changes through the day, so it cannot be deployed as a regime gate.

The fix is the noise-area construction applied to the feature: compare each reading with
its own trailing history **at the same time of day**. `VEI / trailing same-slot mean` is
then a genuine "expanded relative to normal for this time of day" reading, and a fixed
threshold on it selects a comparable fraction of decisions at every slot.

**Why this is not simply a re-run of EXP-0005.** That experiment tested
`causal_slot_percentile`, which is purely ORDINAL — it discards how far above normal a
reading is. The magnitude-preserving members of the family (ratio-to-mean, z-score) have
never been run. This matters because the noise-area programme's own conclusion was that
the per-slot **mean** is a sufficient statistic and distributional reshaping adds nothing,
which points at the ratio-to-mean rather than at a rank.

**Why the direction of the information result is genuinely uncertain.** EXP-0005
concluded de-seasonalising DESTROYS information; EXP-0006 REVERSED that after the warm-up
repair (percentile +0.080 [+0.009,+0.148] vs raw +0.068 with CI crossing zero on NQ).
That reversal is a coin-flip on overlapping CIs, not a settled result. And EXP-0008 just
established that the momentum information lives in the RATIO while the vol LEVEL carries
none of it — so normalising the ratio again is a real risk of normalising the signal away.

## Exact change

- `core/analysis.py`: new `causal_slot_stats` returning the trailing same-slot MEAN and
  SD from strictly prior sessions — the magnitude-preserving sibling of the existing
  `causal_slot_percentile`, same causal window and same fractional `min_obs` policy
  (rule 9a: a strict `min_periods` would delete decisions wherever same-slot history is
  thin, and that deletion tracks time-of-day liquidity — the GC noise-band lesson).
- New `scripts/s5_slot_normalised.py` comparing four features at matched selection rate:
  `raw` (canonical VEI), `rel` = VEI / mu_slot, `z` = (VEI − mu_slot) / sd_slot, and
  `pct` (the existing percentile, carried for continuity with EXP-0005/0006).
- Default window: lookback 90 sessions, `min_obs` 60 — matched to `causal_slot_percentile`
  so the comparison is a single-variable change.
- No change to the feature used by any prior experiment, no trading, no costs.

## Primary metric

Two co-primary metrics, because the claim has two halves.

1. **CALIBRATION.** At a fixed global selection rate, the spread of the per-slot
   selection rate (max − min across the 13 decision slots). A well-calibrated regime
   label selects a comparable share of decisions at every time of day.
2. **INFORMATION.** The count-weighted **within-slot** high-vs-rest momentum contrast at
   matched selection rate — the project's established primary metric (EXP-0005/0006/0008)
   — reported on both markets with session-block bootstrap 90% CIs. Reported alongside
   the **pooled** contrast, which is where the calibration gain should appear (raw VEI's
   pooled selection is confounded by slot composition; the normalised features' is not).

Note honestly: metric 1 is close to mechanical — the construction is *designed* to
flatten per-slot selection, and the unit test already shows it does so on a toy. It is
reported to quantify the size of the fix, not as a discriminating test. **Metric 2 is
the real test**, and its `test_slot_normalisation_removes_a_per_slot_level_shift`
counterpart shows `rel` removes the per-slot LOCATION but not the per-slot SCALE, so
`rel` and `z` may separate.

## Kill test

Declared before the run:

1. **If the within-slot contrast for `rel` (and `z`) is materially below `raw` on BOTH
   markets** — taken as the point estimate falling below ~75% of raw's, or its CI
   including 0 where raw's does not — **the normalisation destroys information** (the
   original EXP-0005 concern), the raw absolute level is retained, and the calibration
   defect must instead be handled by re-labelling the threshold rather than by
   normalising the feature.
2. If the contrast is preserved (roughly flat or better) while the per-slot selection
   spread collapses, the normalised feature is adopted as the **regime label** — a
   measurement/calibration improvement. This is explicitly NOT new evidence for an edge.
3. If the contrast rises materially on both markets, that is interesting but must be
   treated as a THIRD look at consumed history, reported as such, and is not promotable
   without future data.

**Pre-declared regardless of outcome:** this cannot revive Study D. EXP-0004 rejected the
standalone strategy at daily Sharpe ≈0 with a per-trade tilt below cost, and the gap to a
deployable result is far larger than any renormalisation closes. No Null C is in scope
(descriptive measurement; and the standing rule is that a failing real pass does not buy
a null).

## Required controls

- `raw` baseline in the same table on the same common sample (single-variable change).
- `pct` carried forward, so the result is comparable with EXP-0005/0006's normalisation
  finding and its reversal.
- **Both** `rel` and `z`, since the unit test shows they differ in whether per-slot SCALE
  is removed; treating them as one feature would confound location and scale correction.
- Matched selection rate everywhere, so no feature wins by relabelling more decisions.
- Common sample across all four features (the trailing window costs the first ~60
  sessions at each slot).
- Rule 9a coverage table: decisions defined per slot per feature, and what the trailing
  window deletes.
- Lookback sensitivity {30, 90, 180} on `rel` only, reported as SENSITIVITY, not as a
  selection dimension.
- Cross-market NQ + ES; a normalisation fix should transfer, and failure to transfer
  would indicate a tape artefact rather than a construction improvement.

## Multiple-testing note

**This is the third measurement of the same normalisation question on fully consumed
history**, and the answer already flipped once (EXP-0005 → EXP-0006) on overlapping CIs.
The CIs here will be wide and overlapping too. The honest deliverable is therefore the
CALIBRATION result — which is mechanical, high-power, and does not depend on a marginal
contrast — plus a statement of whether information is preserved or destroyed. Any
apparent *improvement* in the contrast is holdout-pending at best. Four features × two
markets, plus a three-point lookback sensitivity, is a small but real search; report the
whole table, not the argmax.

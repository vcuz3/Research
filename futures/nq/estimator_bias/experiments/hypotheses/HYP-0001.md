## Proposed mechanism

OLS on an AR(1) is biased because the lagged regressor shares shock history with the
target. With an intercept the leading term is Kendall's

    E[phi_hat] - phi  ~=  -(1 + 3*phi) / T

— toward zero, for **both** signs of phi, and a **deterministic function of the sample
length T**. The same shape holds for any finite-window estimator of memory (AR(1) phi,
Hurst H, variance ratio): it is pulled toward its no-memory value, and both the bias and
the sampling sd shrink as the window grows.

That splits into two very different consequences, and the split is the hypothesis:

1. **As inference it is usually irrelevant here.** Our IC/reversion statistics pool tens
   of thousands of decisions, so T ~ 4e4 and the bias is ~1e-4. Predicted: nothing moves.
2. **As feature construction it is a live defect.** When the estimate is used as a
   *feature* and its window length varies systematically — with time of day, with
   coverage, with era — the feature inherits a deterministic gradient that is pure
   estimator and no market. A **fixed threshold** on it then selects on that nuisance
   variable. This is the 2026-07-27 learning ("a fixed threshold on a session-reset
   feature is a time-of-day selector") arriving from the estimator side rather than the
   normalisation side.

`futures/nq/noise_vwap/scripts/hyp_0017_hurst_filter.py:100` is exactly case 2:
`H = ghe1(logp[: i + 1])` is a **session-to-date expanding window** — 30 one-minute bars
at the mfo=29 decision, 390 at mfo=389 — and `H_GRID = [0.35 .. 0.65]` (line 60) is a
**fixed** threshold applied at every one of the 13 decisions. `LAGS` (line 59) is
additionally capped at `n // 2`, so the estimator's own lag set changes at the short end
(8 of 9 lags available at n=30, all 9 from n=40).

## Preregistered cells and directional predictions

### Cell A — predicted POSITIVE (the sharp cell)

The expanding-window Hurst gate is partly a clock.

- **A1 (synthetic):** on fractional Brownian motion with true H = 0.50, `ghe1` evaluated
  at the gate's own window lengths n in {30, 60, ..., 390} has an E[H_hat] and an
  sd(H_hat) that both vary materially across n.
- **A2 (real):** on NQ/ES the per-slot selection rate of a fixed `H >= thr` gate varies
  across the 13 decision slots far more than a same-slot z-scored H at a matched global
  selection rate.

### Cell B — predicted NULL

`vei_exploration`'s AC1 pools within-session consecutive pairs across all sessions
(T ~ 4.5e4). Kendall bias at that T is ~ -6e-5. Predicted: bias correction moves AC1 by
< 0.001 and the SMA(-0.01) / Wilder(0.55) / EMA(0.16) ranking is untouched.

**Separate, opposite-signed sub-prediction on the same statistic:** that estimator pools
across decision slots, and VEI has a documented per-slot level drift (0.745 -> 1.165
across the session, FINDINGS §A). Part of the pooled "persistence" is therefore the
shared deterministic clock, which biases AC1 **up**, not down. Predicted: within-slot
demeaning moves AC1 materially downward. Report both numbers.

### Cell C — predicted NULL

`claude_exploration_1` finding (d) reversion retention (31-56% at 5 min, 12-33% at
30 min) rests on correlations over ~4e4 decisions. Predicted: unchanged under bias
correction.

## Primary metric

Per-slot **selection-rate CV** of the fixed-threshold Hurst gate on NQ and ES, compared
against the same-slot z-scored H at matched global selection rate on a common sample.

Calibration for reading it, from `futures/forex/vwap_exploration` finding I1: a
same-slot z-score scores CV 0.060-0.064; RSI's fixed 70/30 cut scored 0.223-0.364, i.e.
3.5-5.7x worse, and that was ruled a time-of-day selector.

## Kill test

**Cell A is REJECTED if** the fixed-threshold gate's per-slot selection-rate CV is
**<= 2x** the same-slot-z gate's CV, at matched global selection rate, on **either** NQ
or ES. In that case the noise_vwap Hurst result stands unqualified on this axis and the
window-length mechanism is not material at our window lengths.

**Cells B and C are REJECTED — and Cell A's interpretation is re-opened — if** analytic
or bootstrap bias correction moves either published statistic by more than 0.01
(AC1) or by more than its published CI (reversion retention). Either would mean the
model of *where* the bias bites is wrong, and no part of this hypothesis could be
trusted.

## Required controls

1. **Rule 23 reproduction first.** Reproduce the published VEI AC1 column and the
   published reversion-retention numbers before auditing them. Four decimal places on
   identical inputs; carry any unexplained difference as a limitation.
2. **Degenerate control (the decisive one for Cell A).** Recompute the per-slot
   selection-rate CV with the real price path replaced by synthetic H=0.50 fBm of the
   same length. If the CV is as large on pure noise as on the real tape, the gradient is
   100% estimator and 0% market — the strongest form of the finding.
3. **Matched global selection rate on a common sample** when comparing the fixed
   threshold against the same-slot z-score (RSI learning; otherwise one variant wins by
   relabelling more observations).
4. **Cross-market.** Every cell on NQ **and** ES.
5. **Two independent bias correctors** — Kendall analytic and a parametric bootstrap —
   must agree, so a correction bug cannot masquerade as a finding.
6. **Sampling-sd arm reported alongside the bias arm.** A fixed threshold is sensitive
   to the estimator's *dispersion* as well as its level, and A1 predicts the dispersion
   moves more than the level.

## Rationale and evidence

The workspace has repeatedly found that a threshold on a normalised feature is really a
selector on the normaliser (2026-07-27 session-reset features; 2026-08-03 RSI's
trailing-window 70/30 cut). Estimator bias is a third route to the same defect and it is
the one nobody looks at, because the estimate is usually *reported* rather than
*thresholded*. The Hurst gate both thresholds it and grows its window monotonically
through the session, which makes it the cleanest available test.

`hurst_explore` A1/EXP-0003 already established the ingredients on the measurement side:
synthetic sd(H_hat) is 0.17-0.30 at n=30 versus 0.04-0.09 at a full 1m day, and
executable inverse calibration was needed to state the NQ/ES panel honestly. What has not
been done is to carry that through to the *decision rule* that consumes H, which is where
a bias that varies with the clock stops being a measurement caveat and becomes a
selection artifact.

Note this hypothesis can only *qualify* the noise_vwap Hurst result, never revive it:
EXP-0026/0027/0028 rejected the gate as selection, as exit conditioning and as sizing.
The value is (a) whether "H level is a real per-trade quality signal" survives as stated,
and (b) a reusable, tested bias-correction module plus a window-length lookup table for
every future persistence feature in this workspace.

## Proposed mechanism

A European (vol-targeted, continuously-held) trend follower whose signal is a
linear filter of past returns has an expected per-period P&L that reduces to a
weighted sum of the return series' own lagged autocorrelations plus a
drift-squared term. If that is right, the empirical "transfer ladder" this
workspace spent six projects discovering (NQ works -> ES weaker -> GC/YM
cost-fragile -> RTY no gross edge -> FX negative gross) is **predictable from two
measurable quantities per product**, and "which market next?" becomes a table
rather than a three-week port.

### The closed form, stated exactly as it will be implemented

Work in **risk units**. For a product, let `dp_t` be the change in the traded
price over one period of the decision clock and `s_t` a strictly causal
volatility estimate known at `t-1`. Define

    x_t = dp_t / s_{t-1}                        (return in risk units)

so that a position of "1" is a constant-risk position. Let the signal be any
linear filter of past risk-unit returns,

    S_t = sum_{m>=1} w_m x_{t+1-m}              (w known, product-independent)

Then, with `g(m) = Cov(x_t, x_{t+m})`, `g(0) = Var(x)`, `mu = E[x]`,
`SR = mu / sd(x)`, and `rho(m) = g(m)/g(0)`:

    E[S_t x_{t+1}] = sum_{m>=1} w_m ( g(m) + mu^2 )
                   = g(0) * [ sum_{m>=1} w_m rho(m)  +  SR^2 * sum_{m>=1} w_m ]

For the single exponential filter `w_m = nu^m` this is exactly the article's

    F_T  ∝  g(0) * ( [ sum_{m>=0} nu^m rho(m) - 1 ] + (nu/(1-nu)) * SR^2 )

because `sum_{m>=0} nu^m rho(m) - 1 = sum_{m>=1} nu^m rho(m)` (as `rho(0)=1`) and
`sum_{m>=1} nu^m = nu/(1-nu)`. The general-`w` form above is used so the same
machinery covers the **EWMA crossover** the article's own benchmark calls for; a
crossover of prices with kernel `c_m` (zero DC gain) is a filter of returns with
`w_k = -sum_{m>=k} c_m`, and this identity is pinned by a unit test.

**Predicted score (the pre-screen number), per product per era:**

    PHI = sum_{m=1..M} w_m rho(m)  +  SR^2 * sum_{m=1..M} w_m

`g(0)` is deliberately *not* included: in risk units `g(0) ~ 1` by construction,
and a cross-product ranking must not be a ranking of price variance in
incommensurable native units (rule 19).

**Turnover / cost.** The article's `E[a U_t] = (2a/sqrt(pi)) * sigma_target *
sqrt(1-nu)` follows from `E|dp| = sqrt(2/pi) sd(dp)` for a mean-zero Gaussian
position with `corr(p_t, p_{t-1}) = nu`. It depends only on the filter span, not
on the market — so the whole cross-product cost differential enters through the
product's own **cost-to-volatility ratio**:

    PHI_net = PHI  -  (2/sqrt(pi)) * sqrt(1-nu) * E[ c_t / s_t ]

with `c_t` the modelled one-way cost per contract in price units, built from the
tick actually in force that year (measured from the price grid, never assumed).

## Clocks and rules (declared before the run)

- **Primary clock: daily** — last close of each CME trade date
  (`sdate = date(t_ET + 6h)`).
- **Secondary clock: 30-minute** — ET wall-clock grid, `x` standardised by a
  causal **same-slot** trailing volatility. This is mandatory, not optional:
  the 30-minute grid carries a deterministic intraday volatility profile (and one
  overnight-halt bar per session), and LEARNINGS 2026-08-03 records that pooling a
  lag-1 statistic across a deterministic profile *inflates* it.
- **Primary rule: single exponential filter**, `w_m = nu^m`, daily span 32
  (`nu = 1 - 2/(32+1)`), vol-targeted continuous position.
- **Secondary rule: EWMA crossover**, fast span 16 / slow span 64.
- **Execution (rules 1, 2): signal from `close(t)`, position established at
  `open(t+1)`, P&L booked on `open(t+1) -> open(t+2)`.** The article's own
  derivation trades at `close(t)` on information from `close(t)`, which is not
  attainable; that version is run **only** as a labelled construction-validity
  check, never as the primary.

## Amendment before the run (2026-08-03, pre-registration, no data seen)

Writing the machinery surfaced a problem with the comparison as the source item
words it. **`PHI(lag=L)` against realised P&L of the same rule at the same lag on
the same sample is an algebraic identity, not a test.** The closed form is exact,
so that comparison returns Spearman = 1 by construction whatever the market does,
and `tests/test_core.py` confirms it agrees to 1e-10. Scoring it would be exactly
the rule-16 failure mode: presenting a non-re-executed algebraic relation as
validation.

So the three comparisons are separated explicitly, and only the last two are
evidence:

- **(A) Identity.** `PHI(L)` vs realised(L), same sample. Exact by construction.
  This is control 1 -- an implementation check, reported as such, never as a
  result.
- **(B) Published form vs deployable result.** `PHI` computed the way a screener
  actually would -- the article's **lag-0** formula from ordinary full-sample
  autocorrelations -- against realised P&L of the **executable (lag-1)** rule,
  gross and net. Non-trivial: it asks whether the published number survives the
  execution lag and the cost the formula does not contain. **This is K1.**
- **(C) Out-of-sample.** `PHI` from era *k* against realised on era *k+1*.
  **This is K2**, and it is the only one that tests the thing the item is
  actually for.

## Primary metric

Spearman rank correlation, across the >=20-product panel, between

- **predicted** `PHI` at **lag 0** -- the article's published closed form, from
  ordinary full-sample autocorrelations (daily clock, span 32, single
  exponential filter) -- and
- **realised** gross P&L per period in risk units of the matched **executable
  (lag-1)** rule through this project's engine,

with a bootstrap CI **clustered by asset class** (FX / equity / metals / energy /
rates). Clustering is required, not decorative: `MGC` and `GC` are the same
underlying at 1/10 size and `MCL`/`CL` likewise, and `ZT ZF ZN ZB UB` are five
points on one curve — the effective panel size is far below 30 and an unclustered
CI would be badly anti-conservative.

## Kill test

The source item's gate, made precise:

- **K1 (primary).** Spearman(PHI, realised) across the panel must exceed **+0.5**
  with a cluster-bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero. Below +0.5, or a CI spanning
  zero, is a REJECT.
- **K2 (screen validity, the one that actually matters).** `PHI` estimated on era
  *k* must predict realised P&L on era *k+1* (4-year eras) **and must beat the
  degenerate screen** — prior-era realised P&L of the same rule, which uses no
  autocorrelation theory at all. A same-era-only relationship is an accounting
  identity, not a pre-screen; if the naive persistence benchmark wins, the closed
  form adds nothing and this is a REJECT *as a screen* regardless of K1.
- **K3 (ordering, reported but qualified).** The source item asks that the
  ranking reproduce `NQ > ES > YM/GC > RTY`. That ordering is `noise_vwap`'s —
  an intraday *barrier breakout* ladder — while the same item's own "traps"
  section says to benchmark against a matched EWMA rule and explicitly **not**
  against `noise_vwap`. K3 therefore conflates two claims and is scored as two:
  (a) does `PHI` reproduce the ordering of the **realised EWMA rule** (a real
  test), and (b) does the realised EWMA ladder itself resemble the `noise_vwap`
  ladder (a cross-family question about whether these two strategies are even
  ranking the same property). K3 is not a standalone kill criterion; it is
  reported with that decomposition.

## Required controls

1. **Construction-validity / invariant check.** On the *idealised* (`close(t)`
   entry) arm with `rho` estimated in-sample, predicted and realised must agree
   to near-identity (Spearman ~ +1, and per-product relative error small). This
   tests the implementation, not the market. Failing it means the code is wrong
   and nothing else may be read.
2. **Degenerate screen (K2's benchmark).** Prior-era realised P&L — no theory.
3. **Term decomposition.** Score `PHI` against its two pieces separately: the
   autocorrelation sum alone, and the `SR^2` drift term alone. If the drift term
   alone carries the ranking, the "trend P&L is a function of autocorrelation"
   headline is withdrawn even if K1 passes — the formula would be a drift screen
   wearing an autocorrelation coat.
4. **Re-pairing null (rule 18).** Score Spearman(PHI of product *i*, realised of
   product *j*) over permutations that respect the asset-class clustering, and
   read the real statistic against that distribution rather than against zero.
5. **Cost / net arm (rules 19, 20).** Tick size measured from the price grid per
   product per year, gross and net reported side by side.
6. **Span sensitivity.** Spans {8, 16, 32, 64, 128}, reported as a distribution.
   Span 32 is the declared primary; the argmax across spans is a search result
   and is labelled as one.

## Multiple-testing note

Declared in advance: 2 clocks x 2 rules x 5 spans x {gross, net} x {in-era, OOS}
is a 40-cell surface. **One cell is primary** (daily / single-EWMA / span 32 /
gross / executable / full sample). Everything else is sensitivity and is read as
a distribution — slope and sign across cells, never the argmax. No cell is
promoted to the headline after the fact.

## Rationale and evidence

The formula is worth testing here specifically because this workspace already
holds the answer key. Six projects produced an empirical ladder at real cost;
if two cheap statistics per product reproduce it, the screen is worth a great
deal. If they do not — and the honest prior is that they will not, because the
`rho(m)` at trend-relevant lags are tiny and noisily estimated while the `SR^2`
term is just "the market went up" — then the result is a clean negative that
retires an attractive-looking shortcut, which per rule 25 is a successful
outcome.

The sharpest known risk to the claim is that `PHI` is dominated by `SR^2`. On a
16-year sample a product's realised drift is both (a) the thing a trend follower
mechanically monetises through its long bias and (b) already visible without any
autocorrelation theory. Control 3 exists precisely to catch that.

## Outcome (recorded 2026-08-03, after EXP-0001)

**REJECTED as a pre-screen.** K1 passed (+0.8656 [+0.6853, +0.9333], null 0.0000)
and is not evidence: the amendment above anticipated the identity problem and the
run measured it — `Spearman(PHI_executable, realised) = +0.9804`. K2 failed:
out-of-sample pooled +0.0268 [−0.0210, +0.1149], and the difference against the
degenerate no-theory screen is +0.0072 [−0.0083, +0.0492], spanning zero. K3's
premise was rejected rather than its result — the EWMA equity ladder inverts
between clocks, so it and `noise_vwap`'s ladder are not the same object.

Controls: (1) identity check passed at 4.16e-17; (2) degenerate screen ran and
was not beaten; (3) **the term decomposition refuted this hypothesis's own
preregistered concern** — autocorrelation carried the ranking (+0.8563), drift
did not (−0.2632, null frac 0.92); (4) re-pairing null 0.0000 on every K1 arm;
(5) cost/net arm reported at two cost levels; (6) span sensitivity read as a
monotone slope, which turned out to *confirm* the algebra explanation.

The durable output is not the verdict but the mechanism: `PHI`'s year-to-year
rank persistence is +0.7133 while realised trend P&L's is −0.0045, so no screen
of any construction can rank next year's trend P&L on this panel.

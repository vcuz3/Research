## Proposed mechanism

EXP-0005 left the mechanism behind finding C **open**: it weakened H_clock (6J's
block profile differs from 6E's with a CI excluding zero) and it weakened the
crude block-volume version of H_liquidity (6J's `ny_pm` has the same CME volume
intensity as 6E/6B's and 2-3x the reversion). The account left standing was
post-hoc and read off 12 cells: reversion sat where the counter currency's home
market was shut, on 3/3 products.

This is the test of that account on four products that have never been loaded.

**Home-market openness, computed before any P&L was touched** — fraction of each
ET block's decisions during which the home market is open, from each currency's
OWN time zone with real DST, using a uniform **08:00-17:00 local, Mon-Fri** for
every product (no per-product schedule, so there are no free parameters):

| | `asia` | `ldn_am` | `overlap` | `ny_pm` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6E Frankfurt | 0.104 | 0.997 | 0.765 | 0.000 |
| 6B London | 0.000 | 0.981 | 0.997 | 0.012 |
| 6J Tokyo | 0.888 | 0.131 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| **6A Sydney** | **0.842** | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.049 |
| **6C Toronto** | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.997 | 0.953 |
| **6N Wellington** | 0.417 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.212 |
| **6S Zurich** | 0.095 | 0.950 | 0.758 | 0.000 |

The four fresh products make **sharply differing** predictions, which is what
makes this a test rather than a re-description: 6C should revert most in `asia`
(its home market is shut all night), while 6A and 6N should revert *least* there
(their home markets are open then). If the ET clock explained the block profile,
all seven products would look alike and openness — which varies across products
within the same block — could carry nothing after within-product demeaning.

**A problem with this hypothesis, stated before the run rather than after.** The
openness ordering already **mispredicts 6E**: it ranks `ny_pm` (openness 0.000)
above `asia` (0.104), whereas EXP-0005 measured `asia` +0.0522 against `ny_pm`
+0.0069. So the strong ordering version is already inconsistent with one of the
three consumed products. I am not adjusting the definition to fix that — doing so
after seeing the data is exactly the fit this preregistration exists to prevent.
The consumed three are reported and explicitly excluded from the primary; the
fresh four decide.

## Exact change

- New covariate `home_open` per decision: 1 where the counter currency's home
  market is open (`core/home.py`), else 0. Causal and exogenous — a calendar, not
  a price.
- Reversion measure: unchanged from EXP-0005 — fade `|dev_vwap_z| >= 1`, entry
  `open(m+1)`, exit `close(m+30)`, **risk-equalised** (P&L over that decision's
  own causal same-slot displacement scale, the equal-risk estimand of rule 12), so
  seven products with four different notionals can be compared.
- Four new products added to `core/data.py` with per-product tick eras verified
  against the price grid. All four halved mid-sample at four different dates —
  6C in 2016, 6A in 2020, 6N in 2021, 6S in 2022 — so **six of the seven products
  change tick somewhere in the sample**, and a cost quoted in ticks is wrong on
  all of them.

## Kill test — written before the fresh archives were loaded

Let `rho_fresh` be the pooled, within-product-demeaned Spearman correlation
between `home_open` (per slot) and per-bet risk-equalised reversion (per slot),
across the 46 decision slots of 6A, 6C, 6N and 6S.

**H_home is SUPPORTED only if BOTH:**

1. `rho_fresh < 0` — reversion decreases as the home market opens; and
2. its **circular-shift null** p < 0.05.

**REJECTED otherwise.** Circular shift, not plain shuffle, is the primary null:
both openness and per-slot P&L are strongly autocorrelated along the 46-slot
clock, and a plain label shuffle destroys that autocorrelation and is therefore
anti-conservative. Shifting each product's slot labels circularly preserves the
contiguity of both series and destroys only their alignment — 46 shifts per
product, enumerable exactly. The plain shuffle is reported alongside, labelled as
the anti-conservative comparison.

**Reported, not part of the kill test:**

- the same statistic on all seven products (three of which are consumed);
- the block-level (4-cell) version, which is the readable form and matches the
  finding-H table;
- per-product argmax agreement (does each product's best block equal its
  lowest-openness block?), p = 1/4 per product under a uniform null;
- per-product Spearman with an exact 24-permutation null.

## Required controls

- **Volatility partialled out.** EXP-0004 showed the block contrast is not a
  volatility gradient on 6E, but that was one product; here the same conditioning
  is applied to the pooled correlation by partialling each slot's mean `rv_60`.
- **Rule 9a on four never-loaded archives**: session geometry, halt emptiness,
  roll and short-session drops, per-block bar coverage, and `rv_60`/scale
  coverage — read before any contrast.
- **Rule 23**: 6E/6B/6J must reproduce their EXP-0005 risk-equalised block
  profiles to 4 dp after the code changes.
- **Risk-equalised units** throughout; native units never compared across
  products.
- **Cost floor per product per era**, built from `tick_size(product, year)`.

## Review after execution

- Reviewer: Claude (Opus 5) self-review, 2026-08-03
- Decision: **REJECTED**, wrong-signed
- Basis: `rho_fresh` = **+0.0526** (H_home requires negative), circular-shift null
  p = **0.7103**. Both arms fail. Against **−0.2342** on the three consumed
  products that generated the hypothesis — the pattern exists only where it was
  found.
- Decisive cell: **6A is an outright counterexample.** Sydney is OPEN during the
  `asia` block (openness 0.842, second highest in the panel) and `asia` is 6A's
  STRONGEST reversion block (+0.0423). Block-level Spearman **+0.9487, exact
  p = 1.000** — the maximum possible score in the wrong direction. Under H_home
  6A should have resembled 6J; it resembles 6E.
- The motivating argmax was a **base-rate illusion**: `asia` is the best block for
  5 of 7 products (71%), so the expected fresh argmax agreement was **1.00 against
  1 observed** — zero information. The original 3/3 should never have been read as
  p ≈ 1/64.
- Volatility partial does not rescue it (fresh +0.0211 → +0.0055).
- The pre-declared flaw held: the openness definition already mispredicted 6E and
  was deliberately not repaired; the fresh data then rejected it.
- Gates passed: rule 23 (6E/6B/6J reproduce EXP-0005 to 4 dp); rule 9a on four
  never-loaded archives (6N/6S materially thinner, 0.758/0.798 bar coverage, and
  flagged as the least powered cells); all four new tick eras verified against the
  price grid.
- Bug found and fixed mid-run: `stats.spearman`'s `n >= 10` guard returned NaN for
  the 4-block cells and the permutation comparison scored `NaN <= NaN` as False,
  printing p = 0.0000 for all seven products.
- Consequence: finding C's mechanism is **open with no live candidate**; the
  panel is now fully consumed, so any further mechanism test would need the sealed
  holdout, which finding K does not justify.

## Proposed mechanism

EXP-0002 rejected the general claim that displacement from a session VWAP carries
tradable information on FX futures, and found the anchor machinery beaten by the
bare trailing return. But it tested VWAP as a *statistic someone might compute*.
There is exactly one moment in the FX day where a volume-weighted average price
is a **contractual obligation** rather than an indicator: the WM/Reuters 16:00
London fix, the benchmark against which a large share of index, custody and
passive FX flow must be executed.

That flow is mechanical and price-insensitive: a manager tracking a hedged index
must trade *at* the benchmark regardless of level. The microstructure literature
(Evans 2018; Melvin & Prins 2015; Ito & Yamada 2017) documents the signature this
leaves — order flow concentrated into the fix window, price drifting in the
direction of the imbalance ahead of it, and partial reversal afterwards as
liquidity providers who absorbed the imbalance unwind.

Three predictions follow, and the last two are what make this falsifiable rather
than a fishing trip:

1. **Reversal**: the pre-fix drift should partially reverse after the fix.
2. **Month-end amplification**: equity and bond index rebalancing concentrates at
   month end, so the month-end fix should show a materially LARGER effect. If
   month-end is indistinguishable, the index-hedging mechanism is dead even if a
   reversal exists.
3. **The 2015 natural experiment**: WM widened the fix calculation window from
   1 minute to 5 minutes on **2015-02-15**, explicitly to dilute the
   concentration of benchmark trading. The effect should therefore be SMALLER
   after that date. This is a dated, externally-imposed structural break that
   nothing in the price series knows about — the strongest kind of test available
   here, and the analogue of the "asymmetric prediction beats both-markets-agree"
   lesson (LEARNINGS 2026-07-31).

## Exact change

None — measurement only, no strategy, no barriers.

The fix minute is computed from the **Europe/London** clock, not hardcoded to
11:00 ET. US and UK daylight-saving transitions are three weeks apart, so on
roughly 15 sessions a year the London 16:00 fix falls at 12:00 ET, not 11:00.
Hardcoding would smear those sessions into a placebo hour. The count of shoulder
sessions is reported.

Per session, with `f` = the ET minute of the London 16:00 fix:

    pre  = close(f)      - close(f - 30)     drift into the fix
    post = close(f + 30) - open(f + 2)       after the fix window closes

Entry is `open(f+2)`: the post-2015 fix window runs to 16:02:30 London, so `f+2`
is the first minute whose open is not inside the widest published window (rules
1, 2). `pre` is scaled by the causal trailing same-slot mean of `|pre|` over 90
prior sessions.

    bet = -sign(pre_z) * post                fade the pre-fix drift

## Frozen baseline

`core/fix.py` and `core/frame.py` at the commit registered in the EXP-0003
ledger row.

## Primary metric

Per-bet mean of `bet` in pips over sessions with `|pre_z| >= 1.0`, with a
session cluster-robust SE and a session block-bootstrap 95% CI. (Each session
contributes at most one fix bet, so clustering is a formality here and is kept
only for consistency of the reported estimand.)

## Kill test — written before the run

REJECTED if any of the following holds.

1. **Reversal**: the per-bet mean is not positive with |t| >= 2 on both 6E and 6B.
2. **Not special**: the fix hour's per-bet mean does not exceed the 90th
   percentile of the same statistic computed at every other candidate hour of the
   session, on both products. EXP-0002 already established a general
   short-horizon reversal on this data; if 11:00 ET is an ordinary draw from the
   placebo-hour distribution, there is no fix effect — only the reversal already
   in hand, relabelled.
3. **Month-end**: the month-end fix effect is not larger than the non-month-end
   one on both products.
4. **2015 break**: the effect is not larger before 2015-02-15 than after, on both
   products.

Arms 2-4 are the substance. Arm 1 alone would be satisfied by the ordinary
reversal EXP-0002 already measured and would prove nothing about the fix.

## Required controls

- **Placebo hours** (arm 2) — the decisive control.
- **DST correctness**: report the shoulder-session count and re-run with the
  naive hardcoded 11:00 ET to show whether the correction matters.
- **Cross-product**: 6E and 6B. GBP is the larger of the two in fix-flow terms
  relative to its turnover, but no directional prediction is made.
- **Costs (rules 19, 20)**: gross and net against the era-appropriate tick.
- **Sign of the pre-fix drift**: report the unconditional pre-fix drift too. A
  benchmark effect should be symmetric in direction, not a one-sided drift.

## Rationale and evidence

- Melvin, M. & Prins, J. (2015), "Equity hedging and exchange rates at the London
  4 p.m. fix", *Journal of Empirical Finance* — month-end rebalancing flow.
- Evans, M. (2018), "Forex trading and the WMR fix", *Journal of Banking &
  Finance* — pre-fix drift and post-fix reversal.
- FSB (2014), *Foreign Exchange Benchmarks* — the recommendation that widened the
  window to five minutes, implemented 2015-02-15.
- This project's EXP-0002: the general reversal exists but is not anchor-specific
  and is far below cost, which is precisely why a *mechanism-dated* cell is the
  right next test rather than another anchor variant.

## Review after execution

- Reviewer: Claude (Opus 5) self-review 2026-08-02
- Decision: **REJECTED**
- Basis: Arms 1-3 PASS cleanly (fade pays; beats 100% of 21 placebo hours both; month-end 4x on 6B). Arm 4 FAILS INVERTED - the whole effect is post-2015-02-15. Diagnosed by entry-lag and holding-horizon sweeps: entry open(f+2)=16:02 London is INSIDE the widened 5-minute fix window (closes 16:02:30). Effect is zero at f+3 onward. Not a post-fix reversal; it is impact reverting inside the benchmark's own averaging window.

# HYP-0002 — Anchor deviation predicts forward return with a REVERSION sign, strongest in illiquid blocks

- Status: tested — rejected
- Created: 2026-08-02
- Origin idea: IDEA-0002
- Builder: Claude (Opus 5)
- Proposed mechanism: No intraday risk premium in a currency pair, plus transitory-inventory FX microstructure (Lyons; Ranaldo 2009) => displacement from a session anchor should REVERT, strongest where liquidity is thinnest. See 'Proposed mechanism'.
- Exact change: Entry-information measurement, no strategy and no exit geometry: feature dev_<anchor>_z, target fwd_H = close(m+H) - open(m+1), H in {30,60,120,session close}.
- Frozen baseline: core/frame.py::build_panel + core/vwap.py at the EXP-0002 ledger commit; no baseline strategy exists or is needed
- Primary metric: per-signal mean pip of fading |dev_vwap_z|>=1.0 at H=30 pooled, session cluster-robust SE + session block-bootstrap CI, reported jointly with the Spearman rank IC
- Kill test: Rejected if ANY of: (1) spread not negative-reversion on both products; (2) |cluster t| < 2 or bootstrap CI spans zero on either; (3) asia not more reversionary than overlap on both; (4) the degenerate `open` anchor lands inside vwap's CI.
- Required controls: degenerate anchors (open, pclose, twap) and the no-anchor past_30 arm; trailing-return partial-out; shared-decision-bar artifact sizing; per-year era stability; cross-product; era-appropriate costs
- Data scope: 6E and 6B, CME trade dates 2010-06-07 .. 2023-12-29 (EXPLORE),
  30-minute wall-clock decision grid (ET :29/:59), 46 decisions per session.
  2024/2025/2026 sealed.
- Multiple-testing note: 4 anchors x 4 forward horizons x 4 liquidity blocks x 2
  products = 128 declared cells, all reported, none selected on. The primary
  metric is ONE pre-declared cell (`vwap`, h=30, pooled); everything else is a
  declared control surface, and the block/horizon reads are interpreted as a
  SHAPE (monotone in liquidity) rather than as an argmax.

## Proposed mechanism

Two structural facts point the same way and predict a *negative* sign, which is
the opposite of what the same construct does on equity indices:

1. **There is no intraday risk premium in a currency pair.** The NQ/ES
   noise-area work monetises a real positive intraday drift on a large-cap equity
   tape; a relative price between two fiat currencies has no such backdrop. The
   workspace's own transfer ladder already found the momentum-breakout family
   goes negative-at-gross on spot EUR/GBP/AUD/NZD (median −0.55 pips/trade, 4/4
   pairs), and the little signal that survived there was weak short-horizon
   *reversion* in the carry pairs.

2. **FX intraday displacement is largely inventory, and inventory is unwound.**
   The market-microstructure account of FX (Lyons' portfolio-shifts model;
   Ranaldo 2009 on time-of-day patterns tied to trading-centre opens and closes)
   is that transitory order flow pushes price away from its average and dealers
   lay the position off. Displacement from a session-anchored average price is
   about as direct a proxy for "accumulated transitory flow" as a bar file
   permits.

The two facts also predict the *shape*, which is the part that makes this
falsifiable rather than a coin flip: reversion should be **strongest where
liquidity is thinnest** (the Asia block, where a given displacement represents
less real information and more inventory) and **weakest or absent in the
London/NY overlap**, where genuine information arrives and price should be
permitted to move.

## Exact change

None — entry-information measurement, no strategy, no exit geometry (rule 15).
For every decision `m` on the 30-minute grid:

- feature: `dev_<anchor>_z = (close(m) - anchor(m)) / scale`, `scale` = causal
  trailing same-slot mean of `|close - anchor|` over 90 prior sessions
  (`min_periods` 45, the rule-9a fractional floor set by measured 6B coverage);
- target: `fwd_H = close(m+H) - open(m+1)`, i.e. entry at the first tradable
  price after the decision bar closes (rules 1, 2), exit at a bar close;
- `H in {30, 60, 120, session close}`.

## Frozen baseline

`core/frame.py::build_panel` and `core/vwap.py` at the commit registered in the
EXP-0002 ledger row. No baseline strategy exists or is needed.

## Primary metric

Per-signal mean of `fwd_30` in the top minus the bottom quintile of `dev_vwap_z`,
in pips, with a **session cluster-robust** SE (`core/stats.py::cluster_t`) and a
session block-bootstrap CI. Reported jointly with the Spearman rank IC of
`dev_vwap_z` against `fwd_30`.

Both views are mandatory, not optional: on GC a rank IC of −0.036 with a CI
excluding zero sat on a mean spread of zero, while ES/NQ had rank IC ≈ 0 with
real positive mean spreads, because intraday continuation lives in the tails
(LEARNINGS 2026-07-31a). A one-view answer here would be uninterpretable.

The session-averaged estimand is computed and printed **only as a contrast**,
together with `corr(session mean, session count)`, per LEARNINGS 2026-08-01.

## Kill test — written before the run

The hypothesis is REJECTED if any of the following holds.

1. **Sign**: the quintile mean spread at `h=30` is not negative on both 6E and 6B.
2. **Significance**: |cluster t| < 2.0 on either product, or the block-bootstrap
   95% CI spans zero on either product.
3. **Shape**: the reversion is not larger (more negative) in `asia` than in
   `overlap` on both products. A flat-across-blocks effect falsifies the
   inventory mechanism even if the pooled sign survives — this is the
   conditional cell that must fire, declared in advance (LEARNINGS 2026-07-31).
4. **Anchor-specificity**: if the `open` and `pclose` degenerate anchors produce a
   spread within the bootstrap CI of `vwap`'s, then the result is about
   displacement from *any* fixed reference and has nothing to do with VWAP; the
   VWAP framing is withdrawn even if the sign and shape survive.

## Required controls

- **Degenerate anchors** (`open`, `pclose`) — the "is it VWAP at all?" arm; also
  `twap`, which carries HYP-0001's information question.
- **Trailing-return partial-out**: `dev_z` is mechanically correlated with the
  trailing return. Report `partial_spearman(dev_z, fwd, past_30)`. If the effect
  vanishes, this is a plain reversal study and the anchor adds nothing.
- **Shared-bar artifact**: already structurally excluded — `past` ends at
  `close(m)` and `fwd` starts at `open(m+1)`, so no price is shared. Verified by
  also reporting the variant that (wrongly) enters at `close(m)`, to size the
  artifact on this data (LEARNINGS 2026-07-31d found it worth 31-56% of measured
  5-minute reversion elsewhere).
- **Era**: per-year sign stability. An effect concentrated in 2010-2012 is a
  post-crisis artifact.
- **Cross-product**: 6E and 6B must agree; they are correlated, so agreement is
  cheap and is a necessary-not-sufficient check.
- **Costs (rule 19/20)**: report the spread in pips GROSS and against a realistic
  round-trip. 6E is $12.50/pip on 125,000 EUR; 6B is $6.25/pip on 62,500 GBP;
  6E's tick halved in 2016, so the cost floor is era-dependent and must be
  quoted per era, not once.

## Rationale and evidence

- LEARNINGS 2026-08-01 sub-lesson (d): the transfer ladder ends with FX spot
  negative at gross for intraday momentum; the residual signal was reversion.
- LEARNINGS 2026-07-31a: report the mean and the rank; they disagree by asset
  class.
- Ranaldo, A. (2009), "Segmentation and time-of-day patterns in foreign exchange
  markets", *Journal of Banking & Finance* — systematic intraday FX reversal tied
  to trading-centre hours.
- Lyons, R. (2001), *The Microstructure Approach to Exchange Rates* —
  portfolio-shifts / transitory-inventory account of FX price impact.

# HYP-0001 - Minute-level decay of AUD triangular pricing signal

- Status: proposed
- Created: 2026-08-13
- Origin idea: IDEA-0001
- Builder: Codex
- Proposed mechanism: a genuine but short-lived cross-rate dislocation should remain measurable after realistic minute-scale delays, then decay monotonically as market makers reconcile the three books.
- Exact change: hold the frozen 30-minute-bar Z(100), ±3 first-crossing signal fixed; enter after 0/1/2/5/10/15/30 minutes and hold exactly 30 minutes after every entry.
- Frozen baseline: 2015–2020, log basis, 30-minute bars, Z lookback 100 bars (50 hours), entry threshold 3, crossing signals.
- Primary metric: three-leg residual mean net basis points per signal at a five-minute delay, on the identical paired signal set.
- Kill test: the capturable-dislocation thesis is materially weakened if five-minute residual net expectancy is non-positive at 0.5 bp round-trip cost per leg, or if five-minute gross retains less than 50% of delay-zero gross.
- Required controls: identical signals across delays; constant 30-minute post-entry hold; exact one-minute timestamp join; coverage/exclusion report; AUDUSD-only arm; annual and UTC-hour breakdowns; delay-zero reproduction.
- Data scope: local AUDUSD, AUDJPY, USDJPY one-minute midpoint OHLC, 2015-01-01 through 2020-12-31.
- Multiple-testing note: all seven delays are descriptive and were named before the material intermediate-delay run. Delay 0 and 30 endpoints were already inspected ad hoc, so this is not clean confirmatory evidence.

## Rationale and evidence

The workbench showed strong convergence in the first 30 minutes and little in the
second 30-minute window. Thirty-minute bars cannot locate the decay within the
first bar. Minute-level entry delays are required to distinguish a capturable
short-lived effect from a shared-close or near-boundary pricing artifact.
